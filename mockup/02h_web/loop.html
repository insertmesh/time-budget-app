You've got time! draft title

Every tap is a bit of data. Here's what it turns into.

← back to the short version
Tap→See it→Decide→Adjust→Tap

See it.

Your day hour by hour. Planned versus tapped. The month at a glance. Untapped time, overtime, sleep versus bedtime. It's a picture of your own time and nobody else wrote it.

The week that felt empty

"I got nothing done this week." The picture shows 14 hours of work, a day of looking after the kids, a doctor's appointment and two evenings of music. Hm.

Overviews show plain words ("25 min more than planned"), never a good/bad verdict.

9 hours is a number

You keep saying you game "a bit". The week view says 9 hours. You can be fine with 9 hours. You just didn't know it was 9.

Decide what you want.

Some things you want more of, some less. Give them a goal. The app labels each goal on its own (grow, keep, reduce) by comparing it with your normal amount. You never have to pick the label.

A goal can be per day, per week or per month. Switch between them and the amount converts for you. Every goal also has two optional ways to end on a date. The calendar only pops up if you add one.

A while: more (or less) until a date

Beach hill

Your holiday is in five weeks and you'd love to be a bit fitter, or at least not die on the first hill.

You set Exercise to 2 hours more per week until the holiday date.

After the date the goal goes back to normal. Or to 0, if you pick that. You don't have to remember to switch it off.

The crunch

A crunch at work for three weeks, and you want to spend less time on hobbies until it's over, without feeling like you quit them.

Hobby goes down until the date, then back to your normal amount.

Deadline: reach a total by a date

The gig in three months

You're playing a live gig in 3 months and you want 50 hours of music making before it.

The app divides it: 50 h ÷ about 12 weeks = roughly 4 h 10 min per week.

It recalculates every week. Miss a week, and with 11 weeks left it asks for roughly 4 h 30 min instead. A bit more, not a lecture. You can also leave it unbalanced and catch up later. Your choice.

Inspection in 3 weeks

The car's inspection is in 3 weeks and the thing you keep putting off needs about 6 hours.

6 h ÷ 3 weeks = 2 h per week of Car repairs. Do it all on one Saturday instead? Then it's simply done early.

Not for everyone, not needed for anything else. Most goals will never have a date.

Friction

Getting going takes time too: setting up, finding the cables, switching from work mode to family mode. Give an activity a friction time in minutes. It's added to every session of that activity, and the tap screen's suggestion already includes it.

The 20-minute warm-up

"Making music" starts with 20 minutes of finding the headphones, booting the PC, and making coffee. With 20 minutes of friction, a one-hour slot isn't 40 minutes of music and 20 of guilt. It's a one-hour slot.

Get suggestions.

The app can suggest changes. You can ignore every one of them.

Laundry takes two hours

Your plan says laundry takes 1 hour. The taps keep saying 2.

The suggestion: "plan 2 hours?" Say yes, say no, say nothing. Nothing changes unless you say so.

Enjoy it.

Sorting out your day at night earns points and a short comment. At the end of the day: a score, a stamp, some confetti. The whole thing is a game. Play it or ignore it.

Homework turned into a game

It's 22:30 and the idea of "checking out the day" feels like homework. Then the app says "Sweet! +1" after your first answer, and you do the next one just to see what it says.

It's just data and we make it visible. We can make it more fun to play with too, but in the end, what you do with it is your choice.
