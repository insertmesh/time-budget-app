House rules

Four rules, with scenes from days you might know.

← back to the short version

1. Your time needs purpose.

Rest counts. Fun counts. Doing nothing on purpose counts.

Two couches

Saturday, 20:00. You're on the couch with snacks, watching the show you've been looking forward to all week. You chose this.

Saturday, 20:00, same couch. You sat down to "check one thing" and it's now 23:15 and you haven't enjoyed any of it.

Both are three hours. The first is Chilling, on purpose, and counts as rest. The second is Stuck, and has a name too. A named hour is data. An unnamed hour is a mystery.

In the app: The Stuck tile names hours that slipped away. Resting on purpose has tiles of its own (Chilling, Family, Friends), so it counts like everything else.

2. Your time is limited.

If you don't know what you spend it on, you will never be in charge of it.

"Where did Saturday go?"

You guessed two hours of cleaning. The log says 45 minutes of cleaning, 40 minutes of looking for the thing you needed, and 30 minutes of standing in the hallway deciding where to start.

That's why the app has Friction: a few minutes added to an activity for getting going. You still did a lot. Now you can see what the lot was made of.

Your own optimistic guesses

"The shower is 10 minutes." It's 35 with the stuff around it. Everyone's guesses are off, yours are not special. They are just yours, so you trust them.

In the app: Every tap is a receipt. The overviews show planned vs. tapped in plain words, and Friction adds the getting-going minutes.

3. Time is relative.

You can't always decide what to do with your time. But without a plan, you also can't adjust the plan.

Forty minutes

40 minutes in a waiting room with no phone battery feels like a whole day. 40 minutes jamming on a new synth patch is gone before you looked up. On paper: identical.

Not all hours are yours

Work, kids, appointments. You didn't pick them, they still count. Put them in the plan as fixed times and the rest of the day wraps around them.

The hobby day

You planned Sunday for your hobby. A friend asks for help moving, you say yes, and Sunday is gone. Did you fix the hobby day's times? No.

Your bad. Learn to fix days for yourself :)

Protecting a day = fixing its times. Everything flexible gives way, and that's by design. It's your choice how much to protect.

In the app: Fixed times in Plans stay put and the rest wraps around them. "Change the plan?" in the morning shifts a broken day for you.

4. Spend it more consciously.

You can't put time in an account. The goal isn't doing more with it. The goal is doing less of what you don't actually value.

The unbalanced week

Your monthly goal is 20 hours of music. Week 1: you made 1 hour (work ate you). Week 2: you caught up with a long weekend. Week 4: you hit 20 hours anyway.

The app shows what each week does to the month, and that's all it does. Leave it unbalanced, catch up later, or fix it now. You decide.

Less of the thing you don't value

You don't want to do "more". You want fewer evenings eaten by the feed. Setting the goal to "less" gives you a number to see, not a rule to obey.

In the app: Goals can be typed or set with +/-, and the week sliders show what each choice does to your month. An unbalanced week is allowed, and Auto-fix only offers.
