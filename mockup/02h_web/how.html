How to play

Four steps. You can use one of them and ignore the rest.

← back to the short version

1. Tap what you're doing.

One tap on a tile starts the clock. The next tap switches. There is no stop button, because time doesn't have one either.

The video about bridges

You sat down at noon to answer two emails. It's now 15:40 and you have no idea how you got from emails to a video about how bridges are built.

With the app: you tap Chilling when you notice. The stretch before that stays on Work until night, when the app asks about it. You answer "Chilling, honestly", or "TBH? No idea". Both are fine.

Three and a half hours of routing

You meant to make music for four hours. Three and a half of them went into fixing one MIDI routing problem.

With the app: tap Hobby, pick "problem solving" as the detail. It was not wasted. It just has a name now, and next time you know that setting up a session can eat an evening.

Pickup at 14:00

Pick up the kids at 14:00. Nobody needs a plan for that. You need one tap when you leave the house.

Tap Commute. Done. The app never asks you to write anything down.

Details like "emails" are optional. "next" suggests the next planned thing, "pick" shows all tiles. A suggestion waits as a grey tile with a slow bar until you tap it. Forgot to tap? The gap is data, you sort it later.

Too lazy to tap?

If everything goes to plan, you don't need to. A plan is a list of what should happen. If the day follows it, the plan is what gets logged. You only tap where reality and plan split.

The boring good day

You planned a normal workday. It was a normal workday. Nothing happened.

At night the app shows one line: everything matched the plan, already approved. Zero taps, a stamp, done.

One tap where it went sideways

Day mostly as planned, except lunch ran 50 minutes long because a friend called.

You tap once, when the call started. The rest of the day still follows the plan. One tap instead of eight.

Chains: when nothing is planned

No plan for the weekend, and you don't want one? A chain is a short, ready-made order of things you already know work well together. "next" suggests step one, then step two, and so on. Every step still needs your tap.

The lunch reset

Lunch, then the dishes, then half an hour of TV. You do this every day, and every day you stand in the kitchen wondering what comes next.

Chain "Lunch reset" = Food (lunch break) → Chores (cleaning, dishes) → Chilling (TV). Tap "next" and the app says what comes next, so you don't have to.

Slow weekend morning

Coffee, a bit of music, lunch. Nothing is fixed. You just don't want to fall into the phone.

Chain "Slow weekend morning" = Food (coffee / breakfast) → Hobby (making music) → Food (lunch break). It's an offer, not a schedule. Chaining small steps also helps when starting feels impossible.

Chains are built in the Categories part of the app. Which chain is suggested when (and whether chains are on at all) is a setting.

Still open: how exactly an untapped, planned stretch is logged is not designed yet. The text above describes the idea, not final behaviour.

2. Plan ahead, if you like.

Plan tomorrow in the evening or in the morning, or skip it. Fixed times stay put, everything else flexes around them.

The five-minute Sunday

Sunday evening, you already know Monday will swallow you. Five minutes of planning is enough: the dentist at 10:30 is fixed, the rest wraps around it.

You choose when the planning prompt shows up (evening, morning, or after your first tap), and you can always skip it.

The plan died at 11:00

You planned a whole day, and at 11:00 it has already fallen apart. You overslept, or the day was just bigger than the plan.

"Change the plan?" offers three things: let the app shift everything (work and fixed times stay), go to Plans and shift by hand, or keep the plan as it is. A plan is something you can change, not something you failed.

Every minute is too much

You feel you need to know what every minute of every day looks like, and the planning itself overwhelms you before the day starts.

Try a block-out instead: Morning = work, 9:00–12:00. Lunch. Afternoon = work, then kids. Evening = free. Four blocks, not forty rows.

Save that as your "Workday" template. Tomorrow you only change what's different. Templates are the reason you don't plan from scratch each day.

A chain you already know works

Every time the bar runs out you think "oh no, what now?" and the thinking itself eats ten minutes.

Turn your best order of things into a chain (see "Too lazy to tap?" above). When a bar ends, the next step is already suggested. You decide once, on a calm day.

Fix only the fixed

Your day has two real fixed points: a call at 10:30 and pick-up at 14:00. Everything else is "sometime".

Fix those two. The rest wraps around them. That's a complete plan.

3. Check out at night.

The app only asks about the unclear bits. Time that matched your plan is already approved.

What happened at 16:00?

At night you can't remember what happened between 16:00 and 18:30. You know it was something. You also know scrolling through your phone for 20 minutes feels like nothing and like a lot at the same time.

Open bits get a short answer: "It was Chores", "Sorry, forgot to tap", a type of Stuck, or "TBH? No idea" (0 points, zero shame). Every answer gets a small comment like "Sweet! +1". Comments can be switched off.

Why check out at all?

Short answer: you don't have to. The longer one:

  • What you gain. Your day gets names. Unclear stretches turn into data you can look at later. Every answer gives a small comment and points, and a finished day gets a score, a stamp and confetti. Thorough days unlock themes and reward tracks, and mini games like "Guess your week".
  • What you lose by skipping. Unnamed gaps stay unnamed, and the overviews have more holes. Nothing is taken away. Points only go up and there are no streaks to break.
  • How to automate it. Time that matched the plan is already approved. "Sorry, forgot to tap" and a Stuck with a reason are approved as well. Comments and points can be switched off (plain mode). Leftovers after you close the checkout go into a short morning check-in.
Two answers, one stamp

It's 22:30. Two open bits are left: 16:00–18:30 and 20:00–20:20. You answer the first one "Chores, honestly" and the second "TBH? No idea".

"Sweet! +1" for the first. "Noted!" for the second, 0 points, no shame. Day closed: stamp and confetti where you tapped the last answer.

4. Sleep has its own tile.

Tap it, close the app, sleep keeps counting. Staying up is allowed. It just shows.

01:20, one last thing

It's 01:20 and you're "just finishing one thing". You said that at 23:00 too.

The Sleep tile shows up at your bedtime, with an orange "+1 h 20 past bedtime" counting along. No alarm, no scolding. In the morning the app asks how you slept and offers to adjust the day.

Why does sleep get its own tile?

Sleep is about a third of your 24 hours. It also sets the start and the end of every day: when you wake up and when you go to bed decide how much awake time there is to budget. A day plan without sleep is wrong from the first line. And sleep is the one thing most people can't tap, because they're asleep.

  • You fall asleep before you can tap. Couch, 21:40, gone. The app asks in the morning on the grey "?" tile: "I slept" with from/to times, couldn't sleep, doomscrolling… Your answer logs how long you really slept.
  • You stay up past the plan. At bedtime the Sleep tile turns up, with an orange clock counting the time past it. Staying up is allowed. It has a visible consequence, no alarm. The next morning offers to change the plan.
  • Wake-up and bedtime are linked. Move one and the other follows (16 h awake / 8 h asleep), or lock one in.
Asleep on the couch at 21:40

You meant to tap Sleep at 22:30 like a person with a system. You woke up at 01:00 with the TV on.

Morning: "I slept" + from/to. Your sleep is logged, and the day isn't a mystery.

Just one more episode

Bedtime is 23:00. It's 00:50 and the next episode just started by itself.

Orange "+1 h 50 past bedtime". Tomorrow: "Change the plan?" and one tap moves the day. No lecture.

Idea, not decided: reading sleep times from a smart watch, so the Sleep tile fills itself. Needs a real integration, so it is parked as a feature idea.

Impatient?

You only need step 1. Tap a tile whenever the day changes. That's the entire minimum. The rest unlocks when you feel like it.
